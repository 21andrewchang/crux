Crux — Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 August 2026

Crux is a climbing training journal for iPhone. There is no account to create, no password, and no server of ours holding your training. This page says where everything you write actually lives.

Your training syncs to your iCloud

Your sessions, attempts, climbs, notes and check-in answers sync through Apple's CloudKit to your own private iCloud database, so they survive a lost phone and follow you to a new one.

That storage belongs to your Apple Account, not to us. It sits in the private database, which means Crux cannot read it, we hold no copy, and nobody here can look up what you climbed. Apple handles it under Apple's privacy policy. Turning off iCloud Drive for Crux in your iOS settings stops the sync and leaves everything on the device.

What never leaves your device at all

  • Every attempt video and its thumbnail. These are files rather than records — they are not in the sync, and they are excluded from iCloud backup too, so footage stays on the one phone that shot it
  • Your height, weight, ape index, pull-up and hang numbers, and your grades
  • Anything you type into search

Deleting the app removes everything on the device. Sessions already synced are removed by deleting the Crux data from iCloud in your iOS settings.

What is sent

Crux sends anonymous product analytics through PostHog, so we can see which parts of the app get used and where people get stuck. That is:

  • Which screens of the first run you reached, and which screens you view
  • Sessions started and deleted, attempts recorded and deleted, and how long a clip ran
  • That a search happened — never what you searched for
  • Your readiness score, and the goal, experience level and climbing frequency you picked during setup — the score only, never the individual check-in answers behind it
  • App opens, versions, and crash reports
  • Standard device context: device model, iOS version, app version, language, timezone, screen size

These arrive as events against a random identifier generated on your device when you install the app. Crux never calls PostHog's identify method, so those events are never attached to a person, a name, an email or an account — because there isn't one. The identifier is not your advertising ID and is not shared with anyone. Your IP address reaches PostHog's servers in the United States, as it does with any internet request, and is used only to derive an approximate country.

No tracking, no ads, no selling

Crux does not track you across other apps or websites. There is no advertising SDK, no attribution SDK, and no data broker. Nothing is sold or shared for marketing, and no data is used to build a profile of you.

Camera, microphone and photos

Crux uses the camera and microphone to record your attempts. Those recordings are written to the app's own storage and go nowhere else. If you choose to save a clip to your photo library, Crux writes that one file and never reads your library.

Crux is not a coach

Crux is a training journal. The readiness score is arithmetic on the five answers you gave it that morning — it reflects what you told it, nothing more. It is not medical advice, it is not a diagnosis, and it cannot know how your body is.

Climbing carries a real risk of injury. What you climb is your decision, and if something hurts, see a physio or a doctor rather than an app.

Your choices

Deleting Crux removes everything held on the device. Your synced sessions live in your own iCloud and are yours to delete from your iOS settings — we cannot reach them either way.

As for the analytics: because those events carry no identifier we could look you up by, there is genuinely nothing on our side to retrieve or delete for you individually, which is the honest answer rather than a dodge. If you would rather none were sent at all, email the address below and we will add an opt-out.

Children

Crux is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Changes

If what Crux collects changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. Material changes will also be noted in the App Store release notes.
